# WICK — a procession treatment for PixelParade

**Logline:** A single pixel flame crosses a sleeping plain of dark tiles, lighting every tile it steps on. One by one the tiles stand up and follow, and the route they march together turns out to spell the tune that called them.

## 1. World and rules

- **Darkness** is an endless plain of unlit square tiles (#06030a to #140820). Asleep, not dead. No sky except while the pads play.
- **Light passes by contact.** Wick lights the tile under each arp note. A lit tile struck by a kick **stands up** as a *walker*: a 1×2 pixel column with a glowing head.
- **Light cools.** A lit tile goes from yellow to amber, crimson and then purple over about 8 s. The route stays behind as a fading trail: the plain is a history of the march.
- **The route is fixed:** one meander of 12 columns whose heights are M1's scale degrees (14, 7, 2, 11, 7, 8, 9, 8, 9, 7, 7, 8). This is revealed only at the end.

## 6. Signature moments

1. **The held breath (f 525 / f 3817).** Everything stops; Wick collapses to one pixel in silence. Then the world opens through Wick's own silhouette. The second time the dark is full of resting lights, and all rise at once.
2. **The answer (f 3614).** On the lone vox tone, a far tile lights *by itself*. It is the only light Wick did not give. It walks over: now there are two lanterns.
3. **The route revealed (f 4941→5077).** Every turn, chevron and loop was a stroke of the tune's own crest. On the final G, small Wick lands on its giant self.

## 7. Feasibility

- **Six components:** `PlainSolo` (sleeping-plain and memory-plain, with a residue prop), `Procession` (first-parade, interlocking and grand-parade, with walker count, camera preset and shadow as props), `Drift`, `Tunnel`, `Homecoming`, plus the persistent `WickLayer`.
- **Tiles:** one instanced grid (about 160×160) with a heat/age data texture, so cooling is computed in the shader. The route is precomputed as a polyline mask with a reveal parameter s(t).
- **Walkers:** instanced boxes (a body plus an emissive head) placed by arc length along the route spline. Hops: a sine on the kick envelope. Beyond about 500 walkers, switch to points.
- **Continuity:** one world-space spline, with each scene owning a segment and a camera; the final crane needs no extra geometry.
- **Hard parts:** the tile-to-walker stand-up (scale over 4 frames; bloom hides the pop), the stair lift (a smoothstep displacement keyed to the 72.0 s run), and crest legibility at small size (bias brightness, minimum 3 px per column).
